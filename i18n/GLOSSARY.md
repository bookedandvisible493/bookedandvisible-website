# Booked & Visible: translation rules and glossary

Applies to all languages: Korean (ko), Traditional Chinese (zh-hant), Spanish (es), Japanese (ja), Russian (ru).
Source language: English (Canada). Audience: independent local business owners in Langley, the Fraser Valley and BC who read this language.

## 1. What the site is
Booked & Visible is a one-person local SEO and AI-search visibility business in Langley, BC. It sells: AI Visibility Report ($79), AI Visibility Boost ($199), SEO Improvement Package ($449), Starter Kit ($129, do it yourself), Landing pages (from $329), Stay Visible ($79/month), Business card design ($99). Flat one-time CAD prices, no contracts, no per-lead fees. It never guarantees rankings, calls or revenue.

## 2. Hard rules (a translation that breaks these is rejected)
1. **Meaning is fixed.** Do not add, remove, soften or strengthen any claim, number, price, timeframe, deliverable, limit or "we do not guarantee" statement. No marketing exaggeration. No new promises.
2. **Numbers and prices stay exactly as written**: `$79`, `$449 CAD`, `5 business days`, `0-100`, `30 days`, phone numbers, emails, URLs. Keep `$` and `CAD`. Do not convert currency. Keep ranges like `$20-120`. You may reorder a number inside a natural sentence, but every number in the English must appear in the translation.
3. **HTML is preserved.** Segments may contain tags such as `<a href="...">`, `<b>`, `<small>`, `<br>`, `<span class="...">`, `<em>`, `<svg>`. Keep every tag, attribute, href, class and the order of tags exactly. Translate only the visible words between them (and `title`, `alt`, `aria-label`, `placeholder` values if present). Do not add tags. Keep HTML entities exactly as in the source (`&amp;`, `&#x27;`, `&nbsp;`).
4. **Segment types**: `title` = browser tab title (about 60 characters or fewer; for CJK about 30); `meta:description` / `og:description` / `twitter:description` = search snippet (English up to ~155 characters; CJK up to ~75; es/ru up to ~160); `ld:*` = structured data text (plain text, no HTML); `a`, `button`, `summary`, `h1..h3` = short UI/heading text. Keep headings headings-length.
5. **Do not translate**: "Booked & Visible", "B&V", email addresses, phone numbers, URLs, domain names (bookedandvisible.ca, ellevenhair.ca, impeccableclean.ca), `llms.txt`, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, YellowPages, Canva, Vistaprint, Staples, MOO, Stripe, WordPress and other brand names. Fictional sample business names stay as is.
6. **Product names stay in English** exactly as written, because checkout and the order form use them: `AI Visibility Report`, `AI Visibility Boost`, `SEO Improvement Package`, `Starter Kit`, `Stay Visible`, `Landing pages`/`Landing Page`, `Business card design`. Short forms used as names (`Report`, `Boost`, `SEO Package`) also stay English when they refer to the product. Describe the product in the target language around the name. Generic uses of "landing page", "report", "guide" etc. are normal words: translate those.
7. **Tone**: plain, warm, direct, professional. Short sentences. Same register as the English. No slang, no hype words the English does not have.
   - Korean: polite formal 합니다체. Japanese: です・ます体. Russian: formal «вы» (never «ты»). Spanish: formal «usted». Traditional Chinese: written Chinese (書面語), «您», Taiwan/Hong Kong usage, not Mainland terms.
8. **Honesty about service language**: pages are translated for discovery; reports, support, checkout and legal documents are English. Where the English says "email us" or "text us", keep it as is.
9. If a string is already in the target language, a proper noun, a number, or a symbol only, copy it unchanged.
10. If something is ambiguous or cannot be translated without changing meaning, translate literally and put `[[CHECK: reason]]` at the end of that value so a reviewer sees it.

## 5. Decisions added after the first translation pass (2026-11-20)
- **Landing page tier names stay in English**: `Single Page`, `Small Business Site`, `Full Website` (they match the order form and checkout). Describe them in the target language around the name (e.g. "Small Business Site (3 pages)").
- **Business card concept names stay in English** (`Clean Classic`, `After Dark`, `Blue Panel`, `Top Result`, `Pin Field`): they label card designs that stay English. Add a short translated gloss only if the English segment itself has one.
- **Punctuation after prices and numbers follows the target language** (full-width `，。` in Chinese/Japanese, normal Korean/Russian/Spanish punctuation). The checker only compares digits, so there is no need for ASCII punctuation next to a price.
- Cited source titles, the street address and the footer address stay in English.
- Owner name stays `Victor` in Latin script in every language.

## 6. French (fr, Canadian French) and Portuguese (pt, Brazilian) added 2026-11-20
Same hard rules as sections 2 and 5. Registers: French uses formal «vous», Canadian usage (courriel, texto, jours ouvrables; avoid France-only slang). Portuguese is Brazilian (pt-BR) with «você», plain business tone, Brazilian spelling (e-mail, usuário, "equipe").
French typography: put a non-breaking space (`&nbsp;`) before `:` `;` `?` `!` and inside « », and write prices French-style when natural (`79 $ CAD`; every digit from the English must still appear). Portuguese: normal punctuation; headings in sentence case.
