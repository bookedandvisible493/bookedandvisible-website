From a one-page site of pictures to a 15-page cleaning website in two days: Impeccable Clean Magic

By Victor, founder of Booked & Visible · Published November 9, 2026

Impeccable Clean Magic had 10 five-star reviews and a website Google couldn't read. Between November 3 and 8, 2026 we gave it a real domain, a 15-page site with prices and service pages, a cleaned-up Google profile and its first directory listings.

Impeccable Clean Magic, impeccableclean.ca

November 3 – 8, 2026; new site live November 4

Own domain, 15-page text website, 6 service pages, prices online, duplicate Google listing removed, all 10 reviews answered

The challenge

The team did great work and customers said so: 10 Google reviews, all 5 stars. Online, almost none of that could be found.

  • The website was pictures of text. Headline, phone, prices, services and service areas were all baked into images. Google could read only the page title and the form labels.
  • One page on a free builder subdomain, with no headings, no service pages and no structured data. The phone number couldn't be tapped to call or text.
  • Reviews were phone screenshots from a Russian-language app, unreadable to Google and confusing to English-speaking visitors.
  • A duplicate Google listing with a typo in the name ("Megic") split the business in two.
  • No directory listings beyond Facebook: nothing on Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp or Yellow Pages.

On November 3 the business scored 34 out of 100 on our foundation check. On Google Maps it appeared in the top 10 for only 2 of 10 local searches (3rd for Airbnb cleaning, 9th for deep cleaning). ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers named it in none of our test searches.

What we did

1. Research before design

We mapped who ranks on Google Maps across 10 local searches, compared prices in the area and studied 9 competitor websites. The gaps were clear: most local cleaners hide prices, don't offer texting and give vague guarantees.

2. A real domain and a new website

  • Bought impeccableclean.ca and redirected the other spellings to it.
  • Built a 15-page site in plain text, with a page for each service: post-construction, move-out, deep, regular, Airbnb and office cleaning.
  • Showed clear hourly prices on the homepage and service pages, with a free-estimate button on every page.
  • Added a service-area page covering the Lower Mainland, with travel fees and minimum visits by city.
  • Made the phone tappable to call or text, and built a quote form that sends leads straight to the owner's inbox.
  • Added structured data telling Google this is a cleaning business, with its services, area and hours.

3. Fixed the Google Business Profile

  • Removed the duplicate "Megic" listing and kept the verified one with all 10 reviews.
  • Filled in the description, services, categories and 14 service areas, and hid the home address.
  • Replied to all 10 reviews and built a review-request kit with a QR code for the team to hand out.

4. Got the business found outside Google

  • Set up Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow so new pages are found fast.
  • Added an llms.txt file and allowed AI crawlers, so ChatGPT and others can read the site.
  • Added Bing Places and Hotfrog listings and submitted Yellow Pages, all with the same details.
  • Turned the old builder page into a "We've moved" notice pointing to the new site.

5. Handed it over

The domains, Search Console and analytics are now in the owner's own accounts.

What other service businesses can take from this

  • If your website is images, Google sees a blank page. Prices, services and phone numbers need to be real text.
  • Show your prices. Most local cleaners hide them; clear prices are a reason to call you first.
  • One page per service wins niche searches. Airbnb, move-out and post-construction cleaning are winnable long before "house cleaning Langley".
  • Clean up duplicate Google listings before anything else. Two half-listings are weaker than one complete one.
  • Reviews are the long game. The leaders AI tools name have 78 to 276 reviews; a steady request routine closes that gap.
