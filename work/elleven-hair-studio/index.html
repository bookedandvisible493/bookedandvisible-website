How a Langley hair salon with 200+ five-star reviews became visible to Google and AI search

By Victor, founder of Booked & Visible · Published November 9, 2026

Elleven Hair Studio had the reputation but not the visibility: 200+ reviews at 4.9–5.0, yet AI search skipped it for two of its three target searches. In three weeks of September 2026 we fixed what stopped Google from reading the site, rebuilt the service pages, brought 45 more reviews onto the site and listed the salon on 7 more directories.

The challenge

Elena Ignatiev opened Elleven in 2024 with one promise: every client sees the same expert stylist, every visit. Clients loved it. Over 200 reviews on Google and Fresha averaged 4.9 to 5.0 stars.

Search engines and AI assistants didn't see that picture. Our AI Visibility Report on September 1, 2026 scored the salon 32 out of 100:

  • Missed on 2 of 3 target searches. "Best hair salon in Langley" and "balayage Langley BC" returned Black Orchid, BAGGIO, Zennkai and Chatters, not Elleven.
  • No structured data. Nothing on the site told Google or AI tools the name, address, hours or services in a format they read directly.
  • Missing from Yelp and Yellow Pages, the directories AI tools check most to confirm a local business is real.
  • Google had stopped reading the sitemap. Search Console showed the sitemap had not been read since September 2025 and had found 0 pages from it. Only 10 pages were indexed; 28 were not.
  • Reviews lived off the site. The homepage showed six fixed, undated quotes; the rest sat on Google and Fresha.

What we did

1. Fixed what stopped Google from reading the site

  • Resubmitted the correct sitemap so Google could find every page again, then requested re-indexing.
  • Redirected a broken /home/ link that Google kept trying to load.
  • Connected Bing Webmaster Tools and set up IndexNow, so Bing and other engines hear about new pages within minutes.
  • Pointed the misspelled domain elevenhair.ca to the real site.
  • Added an llms.txt file: a plain summary of services, hours and areas written for AI tools.

2. Gave Google and AI tools facts they can read directly

  • Added structured data that says this is a hair salon, with its services, hours and location.
  • Fixed a typo in the Contact page address so it matched every other listing.

3. Rebuilt the service pages around how people search

  • A page for each specialty (balayage, keratin, extensions), each with a book-a-consultation button.
  • FAQs, "Products I use" (Kevin Murphy, GK Hair) and salon policies on each service page.
  • A new local page for Willoughby, the Langley neighbourhood competitors weren't targeting.
  • Linked blog posts to the matching service pages.
  • Rebuilt the before/after sliders and fixed mobile layout issues.

4. Brought the reviews onto the site

  • Grew the on-site testimonial library from 11 to 56 written five-star reviews from Google and Fresha.
  • Made the homepage reviews shuffle on every visit, so the page never looks stale.

5. Filled the directory gaps

Listed the salon on Yellow Pages, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Foursquare, Manta, ThreeBestRated and BestProsInTown, with the exact same name, address and phone everywhere.

6. Made it easier to get in touch

Added a WhatsApp card and a clickable Google Maps address to the Contact page, plus a live "Today" marker on the opening hours.

What other salons can take from this

  • Great reviews don't make you visible on their own. Elleven had more reviews than most competitors and still lost searches, because AI tools couldn't confirm the details.
  • Check that Google can actually read your site. One stale sitemap hid the whole site for a year, and nothing on the page looked wrong.
  • Be listed where AI tools look. Yelp and Yellow Pages decided who got named for "best hair salon in Langley".
  • Put your reviews on your own site, as text, not screenshots.
