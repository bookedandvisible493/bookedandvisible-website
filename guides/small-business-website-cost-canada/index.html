How much does a small business website cost in Canada?

Ask three web designers for a price and you might get three answers that are thousands of dollars apart. That's because "a website" can mean anything from a one-page site to a custom build with online booking and payments. Here are realistic Canadian ranges for 2026, and how to work out what you need.

Quick answer

In Canada in 2026, rough ranges are $15–$50 a month for a DIY builder, $500–$3,000 for a freelancer and $1,500 to $10,000 or more for an agency. Budget for the domain, hosting and updates too, and make sure you own the domain whoever builds the site.

Typical price ranges in Canada

  • DIY website builders (Wix, Squarespace and similar): roughly $15–$50 a month, plus your time. Fine if you enjoy doing it yourself and keep it updated.
  • Freelancers: often around $500–$3,000 for a small business site, depending on the number of pages and design work.
  • Agencies: commonly $1,500 up to $10,000 or more, for more custom design, copywriting and features.

These are rough ranges based on published Canadian pricing in 2026; your quotes will depend on what's included.

The ongoing costs people forget

  • Domain name: usually $15–$25 a year for a .ca or .com.
  • Hosting and security: free on some setups, a monthly fee on others. Ask what happens after the first year.
  • Updates and maintenance: plugins, fixes and content changes, especially on WordPress.
  • Email: a professional address at your own domain is often a separate subscription.

What a local business site actually needs

  • A clear homepage that says what you do and where.
  • A page for each main service.
  • Your phone number and a way to book or ask for a quote, easy to find on a phone.
  • Your service area in plain words, and a few real photos and reviews.
  • Structured data (schema markup) so Google and AI assistants understand your business.

Many local businesses don't need more than that. If you're still unsure whether you need a website at all, read do you actually need a website.

Questions to ask before you pay anyone

  1. Who owns the domain and the site files if we stop working together?
  2. What are the yearly costs after launch?
  3. How many rounds of changes are included?
  4. Will it work well on a phone, and load quickly?
  5. Is basic SEO included: page titles, descriptions and structured data?

Common questions

Is a one-page website enough for a small business?

For many local service businesses, yes: one clear page with your services, area, reviews and contact details. You can add pages for each service later.

Should I own my domain name?

Yes. Make sure the domain is registered in your name or your business's name, so you keep it whoever builds or hosts your site.

Is Wix or Squarespace good enough?

They can be, if you're comfortable updating it yourself and set up the basics properly. The bigger risk is an unfinished site that never gets updated.
