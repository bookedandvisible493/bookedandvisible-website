How to handle a fake or unfair Google review

A one-star review from someone who was never your customer feels personal. The good news is that Google removes reviews that break its policies, and a calm public reply often does more for your reputation than the review does damage. Here's how to tell which situation you're in and what to do.

First, decide which kind of review it is

  • Breaks Google's rules: fake or spam, from a competitor or ex-employee, off-topic, hateful, or containing private information. You can report these.
  • A real customer who's unhappy, even if you think they're wrong. Google won't remove these, and trying to have them removed rarely works. Reply instead.

How to report a review that breaks the rules

  1. In your Google Business Profile, go to your reviews, find the review, and choose the option to report it.
  2. Pick the reason that matches the policy it breaks, as specifically as you can.
  3. Check the status later in Google's review management tool, which shows whether it was removed or declined. If it was declined, you can appeal once there.
  4. If it's still up and clearly breaks the rules, the Google Business Profile Help Community is the next step; volunteer Product Experts can escalate clear cases.

If you believe a competitor is posting fake reviews about you or about themselves, you can also tell the Competition Bureau of Canada, which can treat fake reviews as deceptive marketing.

How to reply publicly

Reply to every negative review, fake or not, because future customers read your replies. Keep it short, calm and factual:

  • Thank them, and say you take feedback seriously.
  • If you can't find them in your records, say so politely: "We can't find a record of working with you, but we'd like to understand what happened."
  • Offer to continue offline, with a phone number or email.
  • Never share a customer's private details, and don't argue.

When to talk to a lawyer

If a review makes a false factual claim that could seriously harm your business, such as accusing you of a crime, it may be defamatory. That's a legal question, so speak to a BC lawyer before taking action. Google generally only removes content on legal grounds with strong evidence, often a court order. Be careful with genuine customers: BC's Protection of Public Participation Act lets a reviewer ask a court to throw out a lawsuit over public criticism, so suing an unhappy customer can backfire.

Quick answers

Can I get a negative review removed just because it's unfair?

Not usually. Google removes reviews that break its policies, not ones that are simply negative. Reply calmly instead; readers notice a professional response.

Should I reply to a fake review?

Yes, briefly and politely, while you report it. If it's removed later, your reply goes with it; if not, your reply is what future customers see.

Can I offer an unhappy customer a discount to change their review?

No. Offering anything in exchange for changing or removing a review breaks Google's rules. Fix the problem because it's the right thing to do, and leave the review decision to them.
