The Google Business Profile checklist every local business should run through

Whether someone's searching for an emergency plumber, a dentist taking new patients, or a place to board a horse, the decision happens almost entirely on your Google Business Profile — before they ever reach your website. This checklist covers exactly what to fix, in order.

Why your profile matters more than your website

For local searches, Google leans almost entirely on your Business Profile — not your website — to decide who shows up in the map pack. A half-finished profile with no photos, generic categories, and no recent reviews gets outranked by a competitor with a complete one, even if your actual work is better. The checklist

1. Claim and verify your listing

An unverified or unclaimed listing won't rank reliably and can be edited by anyone — this has to come first.

2. Choose the right primary category

Pick the category that matches your core business exactly. Add secondary categories only if they're genuinely accurate — stacking categories to appear in more searches can hurt trust signals.

3. Fill in every service you actually offer

List specific services rather than leaving a generic default. Specific services match specific searches.

4. Set accurate hours, including emergency or after-hours availability

If you take calls outside normal hours, say so explicitly. Nothing loses a hot lead faster than a profile that looks closed when someone needs you now.

5. Add a real, complete business description

Use the full character allowance to describe what you do, where you work, and what makes you different — licensing, years in business, service area. Write it for a customer deciding whether to call.

6. Upload real photos, regularly

Profiles with recent, real photos — not stock images — get more clicks and calls: completed work, your location or facility, and a face people can trust.

7. Set your exact service area

Define the neighborhoods, cities, or radius you actually serve. An overly broad area dilutes your relevance for the local searches that matter most.

8. Build a steady flow of reviews — don't front-load them

A profile with dozens of reviews all from the same month looks manufactured. A steady trickle after every completed job or visit is a stronger, more durable signal.

9. Respond to every review, positive and negative

A short, professional reply to every review is a ranking and trust signal most businesses skip entirely.

10. Post updates regularly

Use Google Posts for seasonal reminders, new offerings, or promotions. An active profile signals to Google that the business is currently operating and engaged.

11. Keep Name, Address, Phone consistent everywhere

Your business name, address, and phone number should match exactly across your profile, website, and any directory listings. Inconsistencies quietly undermine local ranking.

How long this takes

Doing all eleven steps properly from a blank profile takes a few focused hours, plus a few minutes a week afterward for reviews, posts, and photos. Most of the ranking impact from the initial setup shows up in local search within a few weeks; sustained review flow and posting compound over months.

FAQ

How often should I post updates to my Google Business Profile?

Aim for at least one to two posts a month — seasonal reminders, recent photos, or promotions all count.

Does responding to negative reviews actually help?

Yes. A calm, professional response shows the reviewer, future customers, and Google that the business is actively managed.

Can I do this myself, or do I need an agency?

This checklist is designed to be done directly by the business owner, with no technical or design skills required — it's the same process behind the Booked & Visible Starter Kit.

What's the single highest-impact item on this list?

Photos and a complete service list have the fastest visible impact, but a steady review flow is what sustains ranking over time.
