Google Local Services Ads in Canada: are they worth it?

If you've searched for a plumber or electrician near you, you've probably seen a business with a blue checkmark sitting above the regular search results. That's Google Local Services Ads (LSAs), a pay-per-lead ad format that works differently from a regular Google Ads campaign. Here's how it works in Canada right now, what it actually costs, and where it fits alongside a Google Business Profile and website you own outright.

Quick answer

Local Services Ads charge per lead rather than per click and are open to a set list of home service trades in Canada. Costs vary by trade and city and tend to rise in busy seasons, so they work best as a supplement to a Google profile, website and reviews you own.

How Local Services Ads work

Local Services Ads are a pay-per-lead format, not pay-per-click. Instead of paying every time someone clicks your ad the way you would with a regular Google Ads campaign, you're charged only when someone contacts your business directly through the ad — by phone, message, or booking request.

Eligible ads can appear at the very top of a Google search, above the map pack and regular paid ads, when someone searches for a service like "furnace repair" or "house cleaning" in your area. You set a weekly budget, and Google stops showing your ad once you hit it.

What the Google Verified badge means (and what changed)

Businesses that pass Google's screening process can display a badge next to their listing. Until October 2025, that badge was called Google Guaranteed and came with Google-backed reimbursement for customers if a job went wrong. Google retired that badge — along with Google Screened and License Verified by Google — and replaced all three with a single Google Verified badge. It still signals that Google has checked the business, but the reimbursement protection that came with the old Google Guaranteed badge is gone.

If you're comparing quotes from a business that still advertises itself as "Google Guaranteed," that's outdated branding — it's worth asking what verification they currently hold.

Which businesses in Canada can use them

Local Services Ads in Canada are limited to a set list of home service trades — categories like plumbing, electrical, HVAC, locksmiths, roofers, appliance repair, lawn care, pest control, moving, house cleaning, carpet cleaning, junk removal, tree service, water damage services, and window cleaning or repair are typically eligible. The exact list, and the requirements for each, can change, so check your category directly in Google's own sign-up tool rather than relying on an old list. The Canadian program is limited to these home service trades — professional categories some U.S. businesses can use aren't part of it.

To get verified, Google checks your business licence and insurance, and for "urgent" trades such as electrical, HVAC, locksmith and plumbing, runs a background check on the service professional as well. You'll also need an active, complete Google Business Profile — see our Google Business Profile checklist if yours needs work before you apply.

What they actually cost

Google doesn't publish a standard Canadian price list, and the cost per lead varies a lot by trade, city, and how many other businesses are competing for the same jobs — a plumber in a busy part of Metro Vancouver will generally pay more per lead than a lawn care business in a smaller town. Costs also tend to rise during your busiest season, which is exactly when a predictable marketing cost would help most — the same pattern that shows up with other pay-per-lead directories.

Google's system reviews charged leads automatically and can credit your account for ones it judges low-quality, usually within 30 days. You can also flag a bad lead through the Lead Feedback Survey in your leads inbox, but as of 2024 there's no manual dispute process, and leads outside your service area or for a job type you don't do are generally not eligible for credit — so it's worth double-checking your service area and category settings are accurate to begin with. Check your leads regularly rather than assuming every charge was a real opportunity.

Is it worth it, and how to decide

Local Services Ads can be a reasonable way to get in front of people searching right now, especially in a competitive trade where regular Google Ads clicks are expensive. But because the cost is ongoing and rises with demand, it tends to work best as a supplement rather than your only source of new customers. Before committing an ongoing budget, work out how much you can realistically spend on marketing and what one new customer is actually worth to you.

Common questions

Is this the same as regular Google Ads?

No. Regular Google Ads usually charge per click, whether or not that click becomes a customer. Local Services Ads charge only when someone contacts you directly through the ad, and only businesses in eligible categories can run them.

Do I still pay if a lead doesn't turn into a job?

You pay for the contact itself, not the outcome. Google's system automatically reviews charged leads and can credit your account for ones it judges low-quality, usually within 30 days, and you can flag a bad lead through the Lead Feedback Survey — but leads outside your service area or job type are generally not eligible for credit, so accurate settings matter more than disputing after the fact.

Can I use Local Services Ads alongside SEO or a Google Business Profile?

Yes, and many businesses run both. Local Services Ads can bring in leads while your Google Business Profile, website and reviews build organic visibility that keeps working without an ongoing per-lead cost.
