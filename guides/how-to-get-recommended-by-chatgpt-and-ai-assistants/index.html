How to get your business recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI assistants

More people now ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity things like "who's a good plumber in Langley?" instead of typing into Google. You can't pay to be written into those answers (any ads are labelled "Sponsored" and shown separately). The assistants build the answers from what they can find and cross-check about you online, so the job is to make that information easy to find, consistent and believable.

How AI assistants pick local businesses

Each assistant works a little differently, but they all lean on the same kinds of public sources:

  • Gemini and Google's AI results draw heavily on Google's own data, especially your Google Business Profile, your reviews and your website.
  • ChatGPT's search: OpenAI doesn't publish its full list of sources, but industry testing suggests it leans on Bing's results and on directory and review data such as Foursquare and Yelp.
  • Perplexity searches the web live and cites the pages it used, so directories, local news and your own site all count.

What they have in common: an assistant only recommends a business it can describe with some confidence. If your name, services or phone number look different from one site to the next, or there's very little written about you, it tends to recommend someone else.

The five things that make the biggest difference

  1. A complete Google Business Profile. The right primary category, a clear description, your services, hours and real photos. Our Google Business Profile checklist walks through every field.
  2. The same details everywhere. Your name, address and phone number should match character for character on your website, Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp and the directories. See why matching details matter.
  3. Listings beyond Google. Bing Places and Apple Business (formerly Apple Business Connect) are free, and their data reaches several assistants. Start with Bing Places and Apple Business, then the Canadian directories that actually matter.
  4. Steady, genuine reviews with replies. Assistants appear to weigh how many reviews you have, how recent they are and what people say. How many you need depends on your local competition.
  5. A website that states plainly who you are. A page for each main service, your service area in words, an FAQ that answers real customer questions, and structured data (schema markup) that labels your business type, services and hours for machines.

How to check what AI assistants say about you today

Open a private browser window, go to ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, and ask the questions your customers would ask, word for word, with your town in them. Note whether you're named, where, and whether the details are right. Ask each one twice, because answers change between sessions. Doing this before and after you make changes is the only honest way to see whether anything moved.

What doesn't work

  • Paying anyone who promises a guaranteed spot in ChatGPT's answers. Nobody can sell that.
  • Stuffing keywords or city names into your business name. It can get your Google profile suspended (see why profiles get suspended).
  • Buying reviews or offering rewards for them. It breaks Google's rules and can breach Canada's Competition Act.

Quick answers

Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

Not into the answer itself. ChatGPT and Google now show some paid ads, but they're labelled Sponsored and kept separate from the recommendation. The recommendation comes from what the assistant can find and verify about your business online.

How long does it take for AI assistants to pick up changes?

Usually weeks, not days. Directories, search engines and AI tools refresh on their own schedules, so re-check about 30 days after you make changes.

Does my Google Business Profile matter for ChatGPT too?

Indirectly, yes. Google data feeds Gemini and Google's AI results directly, and a complete, consistent profile usually goes hand in hand with consistent details on the other sites ChatGPT reads.
