Bing Places and Apple Business: the free listings most BC businesses skip

Almost every local business sets up Google. Far fewer claim their Bing and Apple listings, even though both are free and take about an hour together. That's a gap worth closing: Bing's data reaches Microsoft Copilot and other tools, and Apple's powers Apple Maps and Siri on every iPhone. (Apple renamed its tool in April 2026: Apple Business Connect is now part of Apple Business.)

Quick answer

Bing Places and Apple Business (formerly Apple Business Connect) are free and take about an hour together. Import your Google profile into Bing, claim your Apple Maps place card at business.apple.com, and keep every detail identical to Google.

Why they matter

  • Bing Places feeds Bing Maps and Bing search, Microsoft's Copilot, and other services that use Bing's data. Industry testing also suggests ChatGPT's search leans on Bing's results (OpenAI doesn't publish its full list of sources).
  • Apple Business (formerly Apple Business Connect, at business.apple.com) controls how you appear in Apple Maps, Siri and Spotlight. Many iPhone users search there without opening Google at all.

Both also help with consistency: AI assistants and search engines cross-check your details between sources (see why matching details matter).

Setting up Bing Places (about 15 minutes)

  1. Go to Bing Places for Business and sign in with a Microsoft account (you can use your business email).
  2. Choose to import from Google Business Profile. Bing copies your name, address, phone, hours, categories, description and photos, and a verified Google profile usually verifies your Bing listing too.
  3. Check every field against your Google profile and website. Double-check the category, which sometimes maps to a close-but-different Bing one. Then publish.
  4. No Google profile to import? Add the business manually; Bing verifies it by phone, email or postcard.

Setting up Apple Business (30–45 minutes)

  1. Go to business.apple.com and sign in with an Apple Account that belongs to the business.
  2. Follow Apple's steps to verify your organization, then search for your location: claim it if it's already on Apple Maps, or add it if not.
  3. Apple checks the details before your place card goes live; it may ask for documents or a phone confirmation.
  4. Complete the place card: category, hours, phone, website, a short description and a few real photos.

Then do the rest of the list

Once Google, Bing and Apple are done, move on to the Canadian directories that actually matter. Use the same details everywhere, and keep the logins in one place so you can update them when anything changes. Common questions

Are Bing Places and Apple Business free?

Yes, both are free to claim and manage. Apple also sells optional ads on Apple Maps, but you don't need them to have a listing.

Do I need a Mac or iPhone for Apple Business?

No. It works in a web browser on any computer; you just need an Apple Account.

Does this help with ChatGPT?

It can. Industry testing suggests ChatGPT's search leans on Bing's results, and consistent listings across Google, Bing and Apple make your business easier for AI assistants to verify.
