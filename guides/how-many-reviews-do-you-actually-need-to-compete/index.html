How many reviews do you actually need to compete?

There's no universal number — 20 reviews might be plenty in one town and nowhere near enough in another. The number that matters is relative to what the businesses actually ranking above you have, not a target pulled from a blog post.

Why the right number is relative, not absolute

Search “[your category] near [your city]” and look at the three businesses in the map pack. Whatever their review counts are, that's your real benchmark — not a generic industry average. A specialty clinic in a small town might compete fine with 15 well-earned reviews; a plumber in a dense city might need well over 100 to be taken seriously against the competition.

Volume vs. recency

A business with 40 reviews, most from the last six months, often outperforms one with 150 reviews that mostly stopped coming in two years ago. Google and searchers both read a stalled review count as a business that used to be active. A steady trickle of new reviews signals a business that's still operating and still earning trust right now.

A simple benchmarking exercise

01

Search your own category and city

Note the three businesses that show up in the map pack for the searches your customers would actually use.

02

Record their review counts and averages

Write down what each competitor has, not just the top one — the gap between #1 and #3 tells you what “competitive” actually looks like.

03

Check how recent their reviews are

Open a few reviews and note the dates. A competitor with 200 reviews from three years ago is more beatable than their number alone suggests.

04

Set a realistic near-term target

Aim to match the #3 spot's pace first, not the #1 spot's total — it's a more achievable, and often sufficient, goal.

What matters after you've hit parity

Once your review count is roughly in range with the competition, the count itself stops being the lever. From there, how you respond to reviews, how recent they are, and how specific and genuine they read start to matter more than adding another dozen.
