Do you actually need a website?

A website used to be the default first step for any business going online. For a lot of independent local businesses today, it isn't — a complete Google Business Profile can carry more of that weight than it used to.

What a Google Business Profile can and can't do on its own

A complete profile handles discovery, basic information, reviews, photos, and even direct messaging or booking in some categories — genuinely enough for many service businesses to run on for a while. What it can't do is tell a fuller story: detailed service breakdowns, a portfolio, or a page built specifically to convert a particular kind of customer.

When a single landing page is enough

If you mainly need somewhere to send a referral, an ad click, or a “let me check their site first” visitor, one well-built page covering who you are, what you do, and how to reach you usually covers it — without the cost or upkeep of a full site.

When a fuller website earns its cost

Multiple distinct services worth separate pages, a portfolio that needs room to breathe, or content worth publishing over time (like guides such as this one) are all reasons a multi-page site starts earning its keep rather than sitting mostly unused.

The middle path

Start minimal — profile first, a single page once there's a clear reason to send people somewhere beyond it — and grow into more pages only once there's a specific job for each one to do. Building a five-page site before there's five pages worth of real content to put on it usually just means five thin pages.
