How much should a local business actually spend on marketing?

Generic advice says spend some percentage of revenue on marketing — but that number was built for a different kind of business, and following it blindly either wastes money or starves a business that needs more.

Why generic percentage rules don't fit most independent businesses

A percentage-of-revenue rule assumes a marketing spend that scales evenly with size, and it usually comes from studies of larger companies with dedicated marketing teams and paid ad budgets. A one- or two-person local business has a different cost structure entirely — most of what drives new customers costs time, not ad spend.

A better starting question

Instead of a percentage, start with: what's one new customer actually worth to you? A single job might be worth a few hundred dollars — but if that customer becomes a repeat customer or sends referrals, the real value is higher. Work backward from that number to figure out what you can reasonably justify spending to win one.

Fixed costs vs. variable costs

A Google profile, review system, and content routine are mostly fixed and low-cost — set up once, maintained with time rather than ongoing spend. Pay-per-lead platforms are the opposite: cost scales directly with volume, and rises during exactly the busy periods when you'd want costs predictable. Most independent businesses are better served leaning toward the fixed side first, and treating variable, pay-per-lead spend as a supplement rather than the foundation.

A simple way to test and adjust

Start with a modest, sustainable amount of time and money, track where actual booked jobs came from for a month or two, and shift effort toward whatever's actually converting. Local marketing rewards this kind of steady adjustment far more than a big one-time spend.
