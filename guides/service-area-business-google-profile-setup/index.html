Service-area business? Here's how to set up your Google profile correctly

Plenty of independent businesses — trades, mobile services, in-home care — don't work out of a storefront customers visit. Google has a specific listing type for exactly this, and setting it up correctly matters for how you rank.

What a “service-area business” listing actually is

Instead of showing a public storefront address, this listing type lets you hide your address and instead define the cities, postal codes, or radius you actually serve — while still keeping a verified location behind the scenes for Google's own purposes.

Setting it up correctly

  • Mark the listing as a service-area business and hide the address, if you don't serve customers at a physical location.
  • List the specific areas you serve by city or postal code rather than leaving the field blank or overly broad.
  • Keep the service area honest — only include areas you'd genuinely and reliably travel to for a job.

How this affects your ranking versus a storefront

Google still uses a location behind the scenes — typically the center point of your declared service area — to judge distance for nearby searches. A service-area listing isn't a workaround for the distance factor; it's a more accurate way of representing a business that genuinely doesn't have a public storefront.

Common mistakes

  • Setting a service area so large it no longer reflects where you'd realistically take a job, which dilutes relevance for the areas that matter most.
  • Using a PO box or virtual office as if it were a real storefront address — against Google's guidelines and a common reason for suspension.
  • Leaving the service area blank entirely, which gives Google far less to work with when deciding where you should show up.
