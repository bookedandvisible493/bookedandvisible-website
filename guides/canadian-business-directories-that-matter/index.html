Which Canadian business directories actually matter?

Search "Canadian business directories" and you'll find lists of 50 or more. You don't need most of them. A handful of accurate, complete listings does far more than dozens of half-finished ones, and a few directories matter much more than the rest.

Quick answer

Start with Google, Bing Places, Apple Business and Facebook, then Yelp.ca, YellowPages.ca, 411.ca and one directory for your industry. Eight to fifteen accurate, identical listings do more than dozens of half-finished ones.

Priority 1: the big platforms

  • Google Business Profile: the one that matters most (see the checklist).
  • Bing Places and Apple Business (formerly Apple Business Connect): free, quick, and their data reaches Microsoft, Apple and several AI tools. See the setup guide.
  • A Facebook business page, which many customers still check for hours and photos.

Priority 2: the main Canadian directories

  • Yelp.ca: its listings and reviews are shown by several apps and AI tools.
  • YellowPages.ca: long-established in Canada, and its data also shows up on Canada411.
  • 411.ca: a widely used Canadian directory with a free listing.
  • Foursquare: free, and industry testing suggests its data is used by some AI tools.
  • n49: a smaller Canadian directory with a free listing.

Priority 3: industry and local

  • Your industry's directory: the free profile on HomeStars (its paid lead plans are the pay-per-lead model) or TrustedPros for trades and renovations, RateMDs for health practitioners, Houzz for design and building, booking platforms such as Fresha for salons.
  • Your local Chamber of Commerce or business association, if you're a member. These are trusted local sources.
  • The Better Business Bureau, if you already have or want accreditation.

What to skip

  • Directories that want payment before you can list, or that call you with "your listing is about to expire" offers.
  • Sites that don't show up in Google when you search their name plus your town. If customers never see them, they won't help you.
  • Pay-per-lead platforms sold as "directories". They're a different model; see what pay-per-lead directories really cost.

The one rule: identical details

Every listing should show your business name, address and phone number exactly as they appear on Google and your website, down to "St." versus "Street". Inconsistent listings can confuse customers and search engines, so fix or remove wrong ones rather than adding more. Common questions

How many directories should a small business be listed on?

Quality beats quantity. For most local businesses, 8 to 15 accurate listings covering the platforms above is plenty.

Should I pay for a premium directory listing?

Usually not. The free listing gives you most of the value. Consider paid upgrades only if the directory clearly sends you customers.

What if I find an old listing with the wrong phone number?

Claim it and correct it, or ask the directory to update or remove it. Old, wrong listings confuse customers and search engines.
