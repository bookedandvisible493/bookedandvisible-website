Google Business Profile suspended? Why it happens and how to get it back

Waking up to find your Google profile gone, or locked so you can't edit it, is stressful, especially when most of your calls come from Google Maps. Suspensions are usually triggered by something specific, and most can be fixed if you respond calmly and follow Google's process.

Quick answer

Suspensions usually come from extra words in the business name, address or service-area problems, several big edits at once, or duplicate profiles. Fix the cause, gather proof like a business licence or utility bill, and appeal through Google's appeals tool; don't create a new profile.

Soft vs. hard suspension

  • Soft suspension: your profile still shows on Google and Maps, but you can't manage or edit it. Usually a verification or ownership problem.
  • Hard suspension: your profile disappears from Google and Maps. Usually Google believes the listing breaks its guidelines.

(These aren't Google's own terms; Google just says a profile is suspended, disabled or restricted.) Either way, the fix goes through Google's appeal process.

The most common triggers

  • Extra words in the business name, like adding "Best Plumber Langley" to a name that's really "Smith Plumbing". Use your real-world name only.
  • Address problems: a virtual office or mailbox, a shared co-working address, or showing a home address when you actually travel to customers. If you go to your customers, set it up as a service-area business.
  • Several big edits at once, such as changing name, category and address in the same week.
  • Duplicate profiles for the same business or location.
  • Review problems, such as a sudden burst of reviews, or reviews from staff or family.
  • A category that doesn't match what you actually do (see choosing the right category).

How to appeal

  1. Don't create a new profile. A second profile for the same business is itself against the rules and usually gets suspended too.
  2. Fix the cause first. Correct the name, address or category so the profile matches your real business and your website.
  3. Gather proof that the business is real at that address or service area. Google lists business registration, a business licence, tax certificates or a utility bill; photos of your signage or vehicle can help too.
  4. Submit the appeal through Google's Business Profile appeals tool, upload your evidence, and watch your email for the decision. Google warns that documents must be uploaded within 60 minutes of opening the form.
  5. Wait, and don't keep editing. Google doesn't publish a timeline; some appeals are decided in days, others take weeks. Avoid editing the profile while you wait.

How to avoid it next time

Keep your details identical everywhere (see why matching details matter), make big changes one at a time, and never add keywords to your name. Most of the common profile mistakes are also suspension risks. A $79 Visibility Report checks your profile and listings for mismatches like these.

Common questions

Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?

If the appeal succeeds, your profile usually comes back with its reviews. Starting a new profile instead would begin from zero, which is another reason not to.

How long does reinstatement take?

Google doesn't publish a timeline. Some appeals are decided within days, others take weeks.

Can a competitor get my profile suspended?

Anyone can suggest edits or report a listing. Accurate details and proof of your address are your best protection, and if a wrong suspension happens, the appeal process is how you fix it.
