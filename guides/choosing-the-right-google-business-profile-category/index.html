Choosing the right Google Business Profile category

Category is one field, set once, and it quietly shapes which searches you're even eligible to show up for. It's worth more careful thought than the few seconds most businesses give it.

Why category matters more than almost any other field

Your primary category is one of the strongest signals Google uses to match your profile to a search. Two businesses with identical services can rank completely differently just because one picked a more precise category than the other.

Primary vs. secondary categories

Your primary category should be the single best match for your core business — it carries the most weight. Secondary categories broaden what you can additionally show up for, but only help when they're genuinely accurate; padding them with anything loosely related dilutes rather than strengthens your relevance.

A simple process for picking yours

01

Search the way customers actually search

Not internal industry terms — the exact phrases a customer would type, like “emergency plumber” rather than “plumbing contractor.”

02

Check what similarly ranked businesses use

Competitors near the top of your market's map pack are a reasonable signal for which category Google associates with strong performance in your area.

03

Pick the narrowest category that's still accurate

Specific beats broad — a precise category matches fewer searches, but far more of the right ones.

04

Add secondary categories only if fully true

Every category should describe something you genuinely, currently offer.

The mistake of category-stacking

Adding every remotely related category to try to appear in more searches usually backfires — it dilutes relevance for your core category and can read as an attempt to game the system, which Google's guidelines explicitly discourage.
