NAP consistency: why matching details everywhere actually matters

NAP stands for name, address, and phone number — and keeping those three things identical everywhere your business appears online is one of the least exciting, most effective things you can do for local ranking.

What NAP actually stands for, and why Google checks it

Google cross-references your business details across the web as a trust signal. When your name, address, and phone number match everywhere, it reinforces that you're a real, stable, findable business. When they don't match, it doesn't just confuse customers — it quietly weakens the confidence Google places in your listing.

Where inconsistencies usually creep in

01

Old directory listings

A listing created years ago on a directory you forgot existed, with an old address or a number that's since changed.

02

Abbreviation differences

“St.” vs “Street,” “Ave” vs “Avenue” — small, but enough for automated matching to flag as inconsistent.

03

Phone number formatting

A different format (with or without a country code, dashes vs. dots) across your website, profile, and directories.

04

A move you didn't fully update

The address changed on your website and Google profile, but a handful of older directory listings still show the previous one.

How to find every place your business is listed

Search your business name in quotes, and separately, your old address or previous phone number if you've moved. Both will surface listings you may have forgotten about. Google Business Profile also occasionally flags suggested edits from third parties — worth checking periodically, since anyone can propose a change to basic info.

Fixing it once vs. checking it forever

Once you've cleaned up what exists, treat a NAP check like closing the books — a quick pass every few months, or any time you move, rebrand, or change your phone number, rather than a one-time fix you never revisit.
