Can you get in trouble for asking customers for reviews?

Asking for a review is completely fine — encouraged, even. It's how you ask, and who you ask, that can turn a good practice into a policy violation.

What's clearly allowed

  • Asking every customer, not a filtered subset, for a review.
  • Providing a direct link to make leaving a review easier.
  • Asking in person, by text, or by email, as long as it's not tied to any incentive.

What's not allowed

  • Review-gating — asking happy customers to leave a public review while quietly routing unhappy ones to a private feedback form instead.
  • Offering a discount, freebie, or any incentive in exchange for a review, positive or otherwise.
  • Writing reviews yourself, or paying or asking someone to write one who wasn't actually a customer.

Why review-gating specifically backfires

It's tempting because it feels like damage control, but it violates Google's guidelines directly, and it quietly skews your review profile toward looking unrealistically positive — something both customers and Google's own systems can pick up on. It also risks the review functionality on your profile being restricted if flagged.

The safe, simple standard

Ask everyone, ask the same way, and let the reviews land where they land. A profile with a few honest critical reviews mixed into mostly positive ones reads as more credible than a suspiciously perfect one — and it keeps you clearly on the right side of the rules.
