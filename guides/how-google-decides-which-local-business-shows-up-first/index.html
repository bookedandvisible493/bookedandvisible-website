How Google decides which local business shows up first

When someone searches “plumber near me” or “dentist near me,” the handful of results in the map at the top of the page — the local pack — get almost all the clicks. Getting into it matters more than almost anything else you could do for visibility. Here's what actually decides who's in it.

The three factors Google weighs

Google has said publicly that local ranking comes down to three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches what someone searched for. Distance is how close you are to the searcher (or to the area you've told Google you serve). Prominence is a catch-all for how well-known and trusted your business appears to be, based on signals like reviews, mentions, and how complete your profile is.

None of the three works alone. A perfectly relevant, nearby business with a thin, unreviewed profile still loses to a slightly farther one that looks established and well cared for.

Relevance: matching the search, not just the industry

Relevance comes from specifics — your primary category, the services you've listed, and the words in your business description and posts. A profile that just says “general contractor” matches fewer searches than one that also lists the specific services people actually search for, like water heater installation or drain cleaning.

Distance: not something you can trick

Distance is based on where you're physically located, or the service area you've defined for a service-area business. Stuffing a city name into your business name to try to rank there, or listing a fake address, both violate Google's guidelines and put your listing at risk of suspension. The honest fix is setting your service area accurately, not gaming the location field.

Prominence: the one you can actually move

This is where day-to-day effort pays off. Prominence builds from a steady flow of reviews, a complete and accurate profile, recent photos, regular posts, and other places online that mention your business consistently. Unlike relevance and distance, prominence keeps building the longer you stay consistent — it rewards businesses that treat their profile as ongoing, not a one-time setup.

What this means day to day

You can't out-clever the algorithm on distance, and relevance is mostly a one-time setup task. Prominence is the one that compounds: answer every review, add a photo when you finish a job, post an update when something's worth mentioning. None of it is dramatic on its own — it adds up.
