How to respond to reviews: templates for every situation

A reply takes thirty seconds and tells both the reviewer and everyone reading later that someone is actually running this business. These five situations cover most of what comes in.

Quick answer

Reply to every review within a day or two: thank happy customers by naming what you did, invite detail on middling reviews, and on negative ones acknowledge the issue and move the conversation offline. Never argue details in public, even when a review is wrong.

01

The straightforward five-star review

“Thanks so much for taking the time to write this — really glad we could help with [specific thing they mentioned]. We appreciate you!” Naming the specific job or detail makes it read as genuine, not copy-pasted.

02

The five-star review that's short or generic

“Great!” still deserves a reply — “Thank you for the kind words and for choosing us — we appreciate the support.” Keep it brief to match their energy.

03

The three-star “it was fine” review

“Thanks for the feedback. We'd love to know what would've made this a five-star experience — feel free to reach out directly, we're always looking to do better.” This invites detail without sounding defensive.

04

The angry but fair negative review

“We're sorry this didn't go the way it should have. You're right that [acknowledge the specific issue] — we'd like to make it right. Please reach out at [contact] so we can sort this out directly.” Acknowledging the specific complaint matters more than a generic apology.

05

The unfair or false negative review

“We take feedback seriously and looked into this, but our records don't match this account of what happened. We'd welcome the chance to discuss it directly — please reach out at [contact].” Stay factual and calm; arguing details in public rarely helps, even when you're right.

A few rules that apply to all of them

  • Reply within a day or two — a reply that shows up weeks later reads as an afterthought.
  • Never argue the specifics in public, even when the review is inaccurate; move it to a direct conversation.
  • Keep the tone consistent with how you'd actually talk to a customer in person — templates are a starting point, not a script to paste verbatim every time.
