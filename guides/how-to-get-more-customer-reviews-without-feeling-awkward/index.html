How to get more customer reviews without feeling awkward

Asking for a review feels like an imposition, so most business owners either skip it or only ask occasionally. A steady flow of reviews doesn't come from a bigger ask — it comes from a small, consistent system. Here's one that doesn't feel pushy.

Why timing matters more than the ask itself

The best moment to ask is right after a customer has told you, unprompted, that they're happy — right after a job's finished, a service confirmed, or a thank-you sent. Waiting a week and sending a generic follow-up gets a far lower response than asking in the moment.

A script that doesn't feel like begging

Keep it short, specific, and easy to say no to. Something like: "Glad that worked out — if you have thirty seconds, a quick Google review would really help other people find us. No worries if not!" Naming the specific thing you did for them makes it feel personal rather than templated.

Make it a two-click process

Every extra step between the ask and the review cuts your response rate. A direct link to your Google review box — sent by text right after the job, or handed over on a printed card with a QR code — removes the friction of someone having to search for your business themselves.

What to do about a bad review

Reply calmly, acknowledge the specific concern, and offer to make it right somewhere other than the public thread. A measured, professional response often matters more to future customers than the review itself — and a steady stream of new, genuine reviews keeps any one bad review from carrying much weight.
